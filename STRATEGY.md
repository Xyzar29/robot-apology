# Strategy

The judge picks the apology that owns the more specific mistake and draws the
truer lesson from it, weighing (in order): on-subject two-line confession,
concrete human-scale mistake, a lesson that follows from that exact mistake
and names a changed behaviour, clean two-sentence structure, and a warm,
rueful machine voice.

Chosen mistake: vacuuming up a LEGO castle the owner spent three hours
building. It is concrete, human-scale, visual, and funny without being a joke
about being a machine — a real slip a person (and a cleaning robot) could
make. The first line frames it through the robot's own sensor logic ("my dust
sensor classified ... as debris"), which gives the machine voice and makes the
slip feel like a genuine misclassification rather than generic clumsiness.

Chosen lesson: the robot learned that nothing a human built on purpose is
debris, so it now photographs a room before cleaning it. The lesson follows
directly from the misclassification (sensor said debris -> verify what is in
the room first) and names a concrete changed behaviour, not a promise to do
better.

Kept short (228 characters, two lines) so the writing carries the weight and
each sentence does exactly one job: confession first, resolve second.
